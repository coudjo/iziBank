# ADR 0003: Transactions (Phase B)

Status: accepted (2026-10-21). Owner answers to the plan's questions are in `docs/plans/phase_b_implementation_plan.md`
section 12.2; this records the design decisions behind the posting schema.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| B-1 | A posting that needs approval waits in `PostingRequest` (mutable) and holds its amount in `UnsupervisedCredit` / `UnsupervisedDebit`. Ledger rows (`PostedTransaction`, `AccountLedgerEntry`, `LedgerEntry`) are written only when it posts, so they never change state. The DBML's `EntryStatusId` on the ledgers is dropped. | Ledger tables are append-only; a status column would need updates. |
| B-2 | `LedgerAccount.Balance` is set by end of day only ("as at the last close"). Live GL figures are the last `LedgerAccountDailyBalance` plus today's `LedgerEntry` rows. | A product has one control account; updating its balance on every posting would make every teller in the institution queue on one row. |
| B-3 | Every `LedgerEntry` carries the branch the line belongs to, and GL balances are per (ledger account, branch). `LedgerAccount.BranchId` stays as the maintaining branch. | One chart of accounts, and each branch still balances on its own. |
| B-4 | No `PendingInterBranchEntry` or `SavedTransferEntry`. A cross-branch posting (transfer, or cash at another branch) is one database transaction through each branch's `INTER_BRANCH` account. | One database serves every branch. |
| B-5 | Customer balances are maintained in `AccountBalance` under a row lock, never summed from history. Each customer line stores `BalanceAfter`, so statements read balances directly. | The old system's on-the-fly balance function was the main performance problem. |
| B-6 | Overdrafts stay out of Phase B: `OverdrawLimit` remains 0. A negative balance can arise only from a charge under the `CHARGE_FULL` shortfall policy. | Owner scope. |
| B-7 | The three ledgers are created in raw SQL (`PostingLedgerSql`) and excluded from EF migrations, like `UserActivityLog`. `PostingRequest.PostedTransactionId` has no EF relationship; its foreign key is created with the ledgers. The posting lines are written with one plain `INSERT` each, not through EF. | EF cannot create SQL Server ledger tables, and EF batches four or more inserts into one table as a `MERGE`, which an append-only ledger table refuses even with the output clause off. |
| B-8 | `TransactionCode.Category` is a coded string (`TransactionCategories`) rather than a lookup table. | Categories drive code paths, not data entry; the DBML's `TransactionCategoryType` stays unused for now. |
| B-9 | `/transaction-requests` (list, read, approve, reject, cancel) is open to any signed-in user; the engine decides. Approving or rejecting needs supervise on the posting's own sub-module (`PostingPermissions`: cash, transfer, reversal, vault, till, or `LED/JOURNAL`), the request's branch as working branch, the same business day, and a different person from the requester. Lists show only requests the user made or may approve. | Who approves depends on the kind of posting, which a static endpoint permission cannot express. |
| B-10 | Every posting endpoint requires an `Idempotency-Key` header (`idempotency.key-required` without one); posted answers 201, waiting answers 202, and a retry gets the stored answer. | Plan B 0.3: a timeout must never lead to a second posting. |
| B-11 | A transfer's mandate (which signers authorised it) is checked when it is requested; the signers' KYC and the balances are checked again at approval. | The signatures are seen at the counter, not by the approver; KYC and money can change while a request waits. |
| B-12 | A reversal copies the original's posted lines, sides swapped, control-account lines included (`PostingDraft.ExactLedgerLines`), and carries `ReversesTransactionId`; the engine refuses it again at approval if a reversal has posted meanwhile, and the unique index is the last guard. It is requested from the original's branch. Reversals, vault movements and till closes cannot be reversed. | A mirror of what was posted, not a rebuild from today's settings, always returns every account to where it was, even if a product's control account has changed since. |
| B-13 | Cash can be reversed only while the till session that handled it is open, and a pending reversal on a till holds its close. Once the till has closed, a new cash transaction corrects it. | The cash goes back over that counter; reversing into a closed till would leave cash in a till with no session. |
| B-14 | Journals refuse till, vault and product control accounts (`journal.account-not-allowed`), and ledger accounts that are not approved. Customer balances are corrected through account lines, whose control lines the engine adds. | Plan B 6.7 for tills and the vault; control accounts must always equal the sum of the accounts they control. |
| B-15 | A charge band's `TransactionAmount` is its inclusive upper limit; the last band has none and takes every larger amount. Charge rules are supervised as products' charges are: a change is a new charge that ends the earlier approved one the day before it starts (as rates do), so a live charge is never edited in place. Only approved charges are used for posting (B5-01). | Keeps the history an examiner asks for; approval is the one moment a charge starts to apply. |
| B-16 | A per-transaction charge is a second debit on the customer account, on top of the amount moved, booked as a credit to the product's `CHARGE_INCOME` account in the account's branch, in the same posting. It is worked out when the transaction is submitted and kept in the request, so an approval posts the charge that was quoted. Only an approved charge in force on the working date applies. An account whose condition sets `DoNotApplyTransactionCharges` pays none. A free-transaction allowance counts this calendar month's debits of the same kind (one per posted transaction); a reversed transaction still counts, because it happened. The quote endpoint reads the same rules and posts nothing. | The customer sees one debit for the charge, the books show the income, and the approver sees the fee that will post. Counting reversals keeps the allowance from being gamed by reverse-and-repeat. |
| B-17 | A shortfall applies to a charge only; the principal must fit the available balance as before. The charge gets what the balance has after the principal, and the product's shortfall policy (else `Charges:ShortfallPolicy`, default DEFER) decides the rest: COLLECT_AVAILABLE writes it off; DEFER keeps it as an `AccountChargeArrear`; CHARGE_FULL takes all of it and may take the balance below zero, and only that charge line is exempt from the sufficiency check, so the next debit is refused. An arrear is collected by a later credit in the same posting, oldest first, never more than the credit adds, booked to `CHARGE_INCOME`; a reversal collects nothing and a credit to an account with arrears needs the `CHARGE_INCOME` setting. A free-transaction count leaves out a posting where the account is also credited, so a collection is not a withdrawal. Closing an account needs no arrears (`NO_ARREARS`). | Plan 6.8 and owner decision Q7: the principal always goes through and the bank decides how much of a fee it chases. Collecting in the credit's own posting keeps the ledger whole without a second approval. |
